How our trainer works

A soldier puts on a Meta Quest headset and a WHOOP band, then plays one realistic mission. While he plays, we track his heart rate and every decision he makes. After the mission, the app shows him how fast and how well he reacted under stress.

1 · FeelWHOOP reads his heart rate every second.
2 · ThinkThe Mac laptop collects all the data and works it out.
3 · ActThe Quest headset runs the mission and records what he does.
4 · ReviewThe cloud backend analyses it; the app shows the report.

In order

One session, start to finish

  1. Gear upTrainee wears the WHOOP band and the Quest. The Mac and the Quest join the same Wi-Fi. relay.py is started from Terminal.app.
  2. BriefingFirst 30 seconds: a fullscreen 2D briefing video, made to look like a movie in a theatre.
  3. Live heart loopEvery second: WHOOP → Mac → the Mac works out stress level 0–3 → sends it to the Quest.
  4. Mission playsRadio voices, gunfire, rain, the trip mine. The Quest logs every move and sends it to the Mac.
  5. The test momentThe Communicator is hit by a trip mine. A clock starts. We time what the trainee does next.
  6. Mission endsSuccess or failure. The headset shows his results as an AR overlay.
  7. AnalysisThe Mac uploads the session. The cloud backend analyses it and writes a summary.
  8. ReportThe app shows every metric. The summary also returns to the Mac.

Design choices

Voice agent and future add-ons

How the voice agent works Proposed

  1. On the Quest: the mic listens. The moment his voice gets loud enough, the Quest stamps the time and sends it to the Mac on port 5006. This gives the exact reaction time with no network delay.
  2. Quest → Mac: the Quest uploads the voice recording over Wi-Fi.
  3. On the Mac: speech-to-text that runs offline (for example Whisper) turns his words into text and checks for the key points: comrade down, took cover, need backup.

Why this split: timing stays exact on the headset, the heavy speech work runs on the Mac's stronger chip, and nothing depends on the venue's internet during the mission.

Built to scale: LDF Sketch

LDF (Laser Doppler Flowmetry) measures blood flow under the skin. WHOOP does not have it.

We show it as a future sensor: adding it later would give more accurate stress readings. It plugs into the same place as WHOOP, sending data to the Mac, so nothing else in the system has to change.

Before you press start

Setup checklist

  • WHOOP app: Broadcast Heart Rate is ON. Repo
  • Start relay/relay.py from macOS Terminal.app. Other shells may not have Bluetooth permission and the script stops. Repo
  • The Mac and the Quest are on the same Wi-Fi network, so ports 5005 and 5006 can reach each other. Repo
  • The Mac has internet access to reach the cloud backend after the session. Sketch

Plain English

Words on this page

BLE
Bluetooth Low Energy. The short-range wireless link from the band to the laptop.
Wi-Fi
The local network the laptop and headset share.
UDP port
A numbered "door" on a device. 5005 and 5006 are the two doors our devices use to talk.
Terminal
The Mac app where our Python script runs and prints live heart rate.
PPG
Measures pulse by shining light into the skin. Inside WHOOP.
ECG
Reads the heart's electrical signal. Inside WHOOP.
LDF
Laser Doppler Flowmetry. Measures blood flow. Future add-on, not in WHOOP.
Stress level 0–3
One simple number the laptop works out from heart rate. 0 = calm, 3 = highest.
XR hand tracking
The headset's cameras follow his real hands, so he can hold the virtual gun without a controller.
AR overlay
Text and numbers drawn on top of what he sees in the headset.
Unity
The game engine we use to build the mission.
Voice agent
Software that listens to his radio call, times it, and checks what he said.
